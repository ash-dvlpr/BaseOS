# RG SP headphone pop: investigation and successful buffer-retention trial

Date: 2026-09-17. Target: BaseOS 1.2.1 RG SP, vendor Linux 4.9.170 #2,
2026-06-24 19:15:50 CST. Speaker pop fix remains successful by the user's report.

## Latest result

The subsequent buffer-retention prototype passed the consolidated listening
test: "No exit pop; audio works". It is temporarily active on the RG SP, with
no NextUI changes. See the successful follow-up at the end for final state and
remaining production checks. The assessment below records the reasoning before
that trial.

## Assessment before the buffer-retention trial

The strongest remaining hypothesis is a **transient from changing the analogue
output's operating voltage or impedance during codec shutdown**. The line-output
buffer power-off immediately after the hardware ramp is the most specific
software operation to investigate. This is a reasoned hypothesis, not a measured
localization of the audible waveform and not a confirmed fix.

The evidence does not establish a hardware-only limitation. There is a credible
software approach: retain the line-output buffers in their settled idle state
between streams while allowing the DAC and mixer to turn off. Avoiding all codec
power-down between streams is a broader fallback, with a greater idle-power cost.
Neither approach is acoustically validated on this RG SP.

The user asked to stop repeated listening trials. Do not request another trial
without first completing the independent analysis and a coherent implementation
and validation plan. No listening question is pending.

## What the listening results and traces establish

| Case | Audible result | What was verified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital attenuation at volume zero | Pop | Applied during playback, but continuity through shutdown was not recorded; inconclusive as an isolated digital-mute test. |
| Analogue volume codes 1 and 2 | Same pop | Code 2 persisted through shutdown; frontend reset it to 0 about 116 ms later. |
| Normal NextUI volume 3 | Same pop | Analogue code 4 persisted through shutdown and frontend return. This revises the initial impression that only volume 0 pops. |
| Driver clears LMUTE/RMUTE before vendor ramp | Same pop | Kernel trace verifies both mixer-to-lineout routes muted before ramp and still muted afterward. |
| Speaker amplifier stays off with headphones | Same pop | GPIO259 high; GPIO261 low before, during and after playback. |

These results weaken explanations based solely on the special analogue mute
code, the returning menu changing analogue volume, or speaker-amplifier switching.
They do not precisely place the audible transient within the shutdown sequence:
there was no simultaneous analogue recording. The extra route mute ran during
DAPM shutdown, after DMA stop, so it does not independently exclude an earlier
stream-stop transient.

## Driver and hardware findings

The [H616 manual](https://linux-sunxi.org/images/2/24/H616_User_Manual_V1.0_cleaned.pdf),
pages 542-543 and 565-567, distinguishes the mixer mute from the output buffers.
Muting the audio path need not prevent a transient generated by powering down
the downstream buffer. The register map identifies:

- `0x310[12,10]`: mixer-to-lineout mute controls.
- `0x310[13,11]`: left/right output-buffer enables, mask `0x2800`.
- `0x310[15,14]`: analogue DAC enables, mask `0xc000`.
- `0x000[31]`: digital DAC enable.
- `0x31c[0]`: ramp enable; `[6:4]`: step selection.

Disassembly of the exact vendor Image shows LINEOUT PRE_PMD calls
`sunxi_ramp_event`, waits, then clears `0x310 & 0x2800`. Subsequent DAC widget
shutdown clears analogue DAC enables; its POST_PMD callback then clears digital
DAC enable. The trigger STOP callback disables DMA requests and does not directly
remove the line-output buffers. Codec clocks remain enabled at idle: the live
`codec_1x` clock had enable/prepare count 1 and rate 49.152 MHz.

The vendor ramp is **already enabled and already waits**. It used step 4. Its
recovered table requests 101 ms for the 48 kHz clock family and 110 ms for the
44.1 kHz family. The instrumented game exit spent about 130 ms in the original
ramp/output callback. Consequently, simply adding a generic delay is not an
evidence-based fix for an obviously missing wait.

The [upstream H616 driver](https://android.googlesource.com/kernel/common/+/e224cfeb61fe91956d29f4671ca7f687fa95b269/sound/soc/sunxi/sun4i-codec.c)
places output-buffer enables in an ordinary playback mixer switch, while stream
power management controls the DAC, mixers and ramp. It also requests idle bias.
This provides a concrete architectural precedent for leaving the output buffers
selected between streams. It is not evidence that upstream was tested on the
RG SP or that adopting one difference will necessarily remove this pop.

## One targeted development plan

1. Replace the LINEOUT shutdown portion with an audited equivalent of the vendor
   ramp sequence that **does not clear the output-buffer enable bits afterward**.
   Keep the working speaker-amplifier sequencing and normal DAC/mixer shutdown.
   Do not call the original callback and re-enable buffers afterward: the unwanted
   off transition would already have happened.
2. Preserve the exact clock-family ramp timing and configurable step. Prefer
   verified exported register/clock APIs; avoid new assumptions about private
   kernel structures. Specify initial load, repeated starts, suspend/resume,
   headphone removal and unload behavior before deploying the candidate.
3. Automatically verify that buffers stay enabled while digital/analogue DACs
   power off; stream reopening works at 44.1/48 kHz; speaker sequencing stays
   correct; suspend and unload restore an appropriate powered-down state. Measure
   the added idle power rather than assuming it is negligible.
4. Acoustic confirmation needs an analogue measurement or one consolidated final
   listening validation. Internal register traces alone cannot demonstrate that
   the headphone voltage transient has disappeared. Do not claim success merely
   because the intended bits changed.

If buffer retention fails, retaining the analogue bias across streams remains a
software workaround to assess. A board-level limitation would require evidence
that the transient is outside the codec's controllable path. The present data do
not justify declaring the problem unfixable.

## Failed diagnostic and recovery

The first analogue-mute trial crashed at startup in diagnostic `trace_codec`
logging, before the mute test ran. The added `current->comm` access used an inline
current-task helper incompatible with the vendor's `CONFIG_THREAD_INFO_IN_TASK`
backport. Generated code read SP_EL0 and then dereferenced offset 16; the resulting
process-name pointer was invalid. Matching exported-symbol CRCs cannot detect
this kind of inline ABI mismatch. The original speaker-only module has no such
current-task access.

Recovered using NextUI `reboot_next`, never the normal reboot/poweroff path.
After removing all process-name/current-task access, both direct and default
ALSA start/stop tests passed, and the manual analogue-mute trial completed
normally but still popped. This corrected trial is also rejected as a fix.

## Successful buffer-retention follow-up, 2026-09-17

With the user's authorization, the targeted plan above was implemented as a
temporary BaseOS kernel-module variant, with no NextUI changes. It follows the
vendor ramp-down sequence and timing but omits clearing register 0x310 bits 13
and 11. Subsequent normal DAC shutdown is preserved. PM preparation and unload
release retained idle buffers.

The candidate passed four start/stop cycles across direct/default ALSA paths at
44.1/48 kHz, register checks, a freezer-only PM dry run, playback after that dry
run, unload cleanup, and reload/playback. The dry run does not validate actual
hardware suspend/resume. All 28 imported symbol CRCs match the vendor kernel;
the generated object has no incompatible SP_EL0 current-task access.

The user then launched a game with headphones connected at volume 0, raised the
volume to confirm audio, returned to 0 and exited. Their result was:
"No exit pop; audio works". This is the first successful headphone acoustic trial.
It supports output-buffer shutdown as the source of the exit transient in this
case, although an analogue waveform was not captured.

Final state: PCM closed; headphone GPIO 259 high; speaker amplifier GPIO 261 low;
register 0x000 = 0x00000000, 0x310 = 0x00153d00, 0x314 = 0x00220133,
0x318 = 0x00000000, 0x31c = 0x00000040. Thus the output buffers remain enabled
while both digital and analogue DACs are off.

The active temporary module is /tmp/h700_speaker_amp_retention.ko, srcversion
55FFC102E579F9019B1F335, SHA256
dac2375c0e6e0070e277a912f27b9fd606b64b054ecf9a94ede98523da8d7f16.
The persistent speaker-only module retains its SHA256 listed above; the boot
hook is unchanged. A reboot restores that earlier version.

Source, automated verification and the final kernel log are saved under

idle-power cost, actual hardware suspend/resume, full power-off acoustics and
compatibility with other device/kernel targets. No further acoustic trial is
requested for the successful case above.
